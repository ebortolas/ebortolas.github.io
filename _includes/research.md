I study how massive black holes evolve within their host galaxies across cosmic time. I model the dynamics of galactic nuclei to interpret current observations and to anticipate upcoming electromagnetic and gravitational-wave detections.

#### The path to coalescence of massive black hole binaries

Massive black hole pairs form after galaxy mergers, but they coalesce only after shrinking their separation by about seven orders of magnitude. I study this journey, from kiloparsec scales down to the inspiral driven by gravitational waves, to predict the sources that LISA and pulsar timing arrays will detect.

#### Tidal disruption events and massive black hole populations

A star wandering too close to a massive black hole is torn apart, producing a luminous flare that reveals black holes that would otherwise stay dormant. New facilities such as LSST will soon detect many more of these events, making population studies possible for the first time. I model the stellar dynamics that feed stars to massive black holes, to interpret these samples and learn about the black holes behind them.

<small>Key papers: [Bortolas 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08397), [Bortolas et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03408), [Broggi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05786), [Polkas et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13242)</small>

#### Extreme-mass-ratio inspirals for LISA

Stellar-mass compact objects slowly spiralling into a massive black hole are among the most exciting sources for LISA: their gravitational waves will map the spacetime around the black hole in exquisite detail. I am closely involved in the LISA project, and I study how these inspirals form and how often they occur. They may also be connected to quasi-periodic eruptions, recently discovered X-ray flares from galactic nuclei.

<small>Key papers: [Bortolas & Mapelli 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04581), [Vázquez-Aceves, Zwick, Bortolas et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00135), [Broggi, Bortolas et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06277), [Franchini et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00775)</small>

**Current and future directions.** I am increasingly interested in bringing together the different messengers of massive black hole activity, from gravitational waves detected by pulsar timing arrays and LISA to transients found by time-domain surveys and high-resolution observations with the ELT, to build an observationally anchored picture of how massive black holes grow and evolve with their host galaxies.
